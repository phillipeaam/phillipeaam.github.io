# Scoring rubric

Version: 1.0.0 — 2026-10-10. Editorial decision tool derived from the two repository hiring-review documents. It is not validated psychometrics, a candidate ranking or an interview-conversion model.

## Assignment and confidence

Use integer scores. Select the best-supported band, then its lower or upper integer according to how consistently the stated qualities appear. A score of 0 requires observable absence/failure of an applicable dimension, not unavailable inspection. A 10 requires exceptional completeness for the project's actual purpose, not perfection or commercial scale. Cite visible wording, record fields, source sections or browser observations. Material contradictions outweigh polished prose.

Score each dimension from its own evidence: a deep mechanism can score well technically while its decision rationale or measured consequences remain less demonstrated. Do not add a composite bonus for the same achievement. Report source-only facts as improvement opportunities, not content already demonstrated in the catalog. A concise catalog can demonstrate depth without repeating a full case; linked cases can support deeper review when actually examined and explicitly included in scope.

Confidence:
- High: directly inspected current content and relevant supporting evidence; observations cover the conclusion.
- Medium: inspectable content but selective evidence/observations or bounded ambiguity.
- Low: provisional interpretation with substantial uncertainty. Explain it; never use Low to license invented facts.
- Not evaluated: necessary observation unavailable. A source review cannot establish rendered layout, runtime accessibility or link availability.

## Context calibration

- Commercial: emphasize attributable client/product responsibilities, sustained maintenance and delivery evidence. Company scale does not earn points and later public product metrics do not establish personal impact.
- Independent: evaluate achieved scope, decisions, product behavior and documented limitations. Public experiments and unfinished modules must remain distinguished from released builds.
- Jam: evaluate decisions and delivery within the documented time/team constraint. A supported playable submission and resolved gameplay sequence can be a strong outcome without revenue, retention or downloads.
- Study: evaluate the declared learning/exploration objective, implemented mechanism and verified result. Distinguish personal learning from professional impact; a working experiment can demonstrate a technical outcome without market claims.

Missing optional media/CTAs can be appropriate: record Not applicable in the field checklist. Score alternatives actually presented if they fulfill the dimension's purpose; otherwise use Not evaluated with the reason. Never lower a score merely for omitting an optional asset, storefront or full case. Conversely, optional status does not excuse a broken resource that is displayed.

## Optional overall score

The ten-dimensional profile is the default. Provide an overall score only when useful/requested. Rubric v1 uses equal weights of 10% each: mean = sum(evaluated integer scores) / number of evaluated dimensions. Display one decimal, evaluated dimensions/10 and weighted coverage (10% per evaluated dimension). Exclude unevaluated dimensions and list them. With fewer than eight evaluated dimensions, omit the overall score. Partial-coverage scores must not rank against full-coverage scores as equivalent. Changing weights requires an explicit named rubric version; record the rationale.

A mean cannot override a P0 finding or determine publication approval. Report readiness independently:
- Ready for review/publication: sufficient applicable facts/evidence and presentation, no unresolved publication-critical issue; human approval may still be pending.
- Ready with minor edits: bounded clarity/presentation corrections; no unresolved material claim or unusable essential action.
- Needs substantial revision: major product/contribution gaps, material contradictions or critical defects.
- Inconclusive: missing sources/observations prevent a sound readiness decision.

## Priority and recommendation quality

P0: false/unsupported material attribution, confirmed broken public destination or defect preventing use. P1: major gap in product, contribution or evidence comprehension. P2: clarity, relevance or selective-reading improvement. P3: optional refinement.

Do not manufacture three issues when fewer exist. For every proposed improvement, specify an observed problem and locator, proposed change/text, plausible benefit, dependency and verification. Describe hoped-for professional benefit as an inference, never guaranteed opportunities. A report already containing the needed mechanism supports a copy correction; missing runtime verification or outcome data requires recovery, not stronger adjectives.
