# Evidence recovery interview

Use after reading the current entry, Source of Truth and evidence register. This mode recovers context; it does not promise score increases or authorize record/external-source edits. Rubric 1.0.0 remains the scoring authority.

## Select and present questions

Prioritize material gaps: actual constraints and choices, alternatives/trade-offs, individual versus inherited work, a concrete collaboration situation, maintenance/verification/delivery outcomes, or uncertain technology use. If a mechanism is documented, ask about its missing decision context or verification rather than asking the author to reconstruct it again.

Ask no more than five questions in the initial batch, fewer when sufficient. Each should have one focal gap; optional answer cues are not a demand to answer a long list. Do not turn every low score into a question when existing sources or browser inspection can resolve the issue.

### Reusable question format

**Q[number] — [Neutral question about a specific unresolved situation]**
- **Gap / dimension:** [What the sources do not establish; relevant rubric dimensions.]
- **Useful answer:** [Situation, approximate project phase/variant, personal action, observed consequence and available non-confidential support, as relevant.]
- **Evidence needed:** [What can be used as personal recollection; which technical or outcome claims require corroboration.]

Tell the author that “I don't remember”, “I'm unsure” and “that belonged to another project” are valid. Avoid assumed successful releases, performance gains, formal leadership or executed tests. Ask what happened before asking for proof of an assumed event. Do not require metrics when a credible qualitative outcome fits the claim.

Do not solicit private code, pupil data, credentials or restricted records. An approximate period, anonymized account or reference to an authorized/public artifact may suffice. Do not imply that old private-code access is necessary. Ask for corroboration only when it materially affects publication.

## Classify answers before drafting

Split an answer into claims when evidence differs. One anecdote can contain memory, technical facts and an unsupported outcome. Preserve the author's uncertainty rather than sharpening tentative words into certainty.

| Class | Permitted use / boundary |
| --- | --- |
| Source-supported technical fact | Attribute only the documented mechanism, project/variant and period; dependency use is not dependency authorship. |
| Personal recollection | Supports memory, responsibilities, motivation and retrospective context; does not independently establish exact implementation, measured improvement or collective authorship. |
| Corroborated qualitative outcome | Describe the supported observation and attribution, including who checked it and context when relevant; do not generalize one check to all builds. |
| Measured outcome | Preserve method, baseline, scope, denominator and attribution. A recalled number without measurement support remains recollection. |
| Editorial interpretation | Mark inference/reflection; plausible benefit is not observed historical intent or outcome. |
| Unresolved or conflicting claim | Retain competing source/variant information and defer the material claim. A newer answer does not silently override technical evidence. |

### Optional answer ledger

Use in the response or an authorized local evidence register; do not create a persistent report by default.

| Question / claim | Answer and certainty | Class / source locator | Project, variant, period and personal scope | Conflict / corroboration needed | Safe proposed wording or defer decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Follow up only on useful evidence or publication-relevant ambiguity. A responsibility can be described without a metric; universal correctness or causal business impact cannot be established by confident recollection alone. Updating an external SOT requires explicit authorization.

## Technology candidate inventory

When requested, inspect all potentially relevant source mentions, including historical notes and multi-project material. Deduplicate canonical names while retaining conflicts and variant distinctions. Repeated résumé copies are not independent corroboration.

| Canonical name | Source locator | Evidence type | Specific project / variant / period | Individual attribution | Publication status | Recommended action |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Evidence types: **observed use**, **configuration**, **dependency declaration**, **personal recollection**, **multi-project mention**. Combine when needed. Declaration is not use; configuration alone is not a successful build/deployment.

Publication status:
- **Supported for publication:** evidence establishes the intended project-level fact. Distinguish already displayed facts from supported candidates. Apply current taxonomy before recommending a chip.
- **Needs confirmation:** project, use, variant, period or attribution is unresolved and material. Ask neutrally or identify a specific recoverable source.
- **Inappropriate to infer:** the proposed fact transfers unsupported information from another project or generic material. New specific evidence can change the decision.

Keep candidates separate from public Technology chips. Distinguish libraries, services and tools from platforms, patterns and native engine APIs; a supported API/configuration fact may belong in prose rather than a chip. Do not use project size, another entry's tag count or declarations as a quota. Product stack and personal integration/authorship are separate claims.

## Reassessment handoff

Distinguish improvements available from existing sources, information newly supported by answers and unresolved gaps. Record edits require authorization. Reassess only when supported information improves what the public entry actually demonstrates, with applicable rendered observations. Questions answered, prose length and tag count earn no points. Explain changes under the unchanged rubric; unpublished facts remain opportunities.
